2. Connect sources without losing credit

Use your Connections Across Sources notes. Put each citation beside the idea it supports. A citation at the end of a paragraph may leave readers unsure which earlier ideas came from which source.

When does a summary need another citation?

Introduce the source when you first use it. A clearly attributed discussion can continue across several sentences without mechanically repeating the same citation. Reintroduce the source in a new paragraph, when you switch sources, or when readers might confuse the author’s ideas with yours. Cite precise passages as needed.

Your own connection or interpretation should be signaled as yours: Together, these findings suggest… Credit the evidence behind that interpretation. A citation does not make a copied sentence a paraphrase; exact words need quotation marks.

4. Quick checks

Other situations: surveys, interviews, and sources inside sources

Your own survey: Identify it as your survey and explain the method and number of participants. Use anonymous participant labels for responses. Do not turn an unpublished classroom survey into a fictional published reference. APA does not treat your own research participants as personal communications.

A private interview or email: APA uses an in-text personal communication citation, such as (E. Rivera, personal communication, May 6, 2025), with no reference-list entry. MLA can include a personal interview in Works Cited and refer to the interviewee in text. Keep participants’ identities confidential when required.

A source quoted in the source you read: Locate the original if possible. If you cannot, acknowledge indirect access: APA (Patel, 2020, as cited in Rivera, 2025, p. 14); MLA (qtd. in Rivera 14). List the source you actually consulted.

Several works by the same author: APA distinguishes years and uses a/b for the same author and year, assigned consistently with the reference list. MLA adds a shortened title, for example (Rivera, “Feedback” 14). Do not confuse two authors who share a surname.

Long quotations: These examples and the builder model short quotations. APA uses a block at 40 words; MLA uses a block for prose longer than four lines. Block quotations have different indentation and final-punctuation rules.

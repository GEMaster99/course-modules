SPAN 3000 · Fall 2026

Intermediate Spanish Conversation
Proficiency Development for Experienced Speakers

3 credits MWF, 50 minutes August 24 – December 11, 2026 Paul Rama, PhD

This course is a transition into upper-division Spanish, and it is built around a fact that is usually treated as an inconvenience: the students in this room did not learn Spanish the same way. Some acquired it through two years of full-immersion service in a specific country and speech community. Some grew up with it at home and have never been formally schooled in it. Some arrived through the standard university sequence. Each of those paths produces a genuinely different Spanish — different strengths, different blind spots, different regional loyalties. Rather than flatten those differences, this course makes them the curriculum. Students bring the content and the context; the instructor supplies the grammatical spine that moves everyone from a serviceable, fluent Spanish toward a precise, register-flexible, academically and professionally credible one.

Who is in this room

Most students in this section arrive with substantial acquired proficiency — typically from LDS missionary service in a Spanish-speaking country, from heritage acquisition at home, or both. This is not a beginner-adjacent course and it is not remedial. The working assumption is that students can already say almost anything they want to say. The work of the semester is making them able to say it accurately, in the right register, with the right regional awareness, and in writing.

Course Learning Outcomes

By the end of the semester, students should be able to:

  1. Sustain extended discourse across past, present, and future time frames with control of aspect, producing connected paragraphs rather than strings of sentences (ACTFL Advanced-Low → Advanced-Mid).
  2. Shift register deliberately — intimate, colloquial, professional, academic — and recognize when a familiar structure belongs to a speech community rather than to the language as a whole.
  3. Identify and correct fossilized structures carried over from a single acquisition context, including English calques and formulaic subjunctive use.
  4. Describe and use major regional variation in address systems, lexicon, clitic usage, and tense preference, without treating any one variety as the default.
  5. Handle hypothetical, counterfactual, and reported discourse — the structures that mark the boundary between Intermediate and Advanced proficiency.
  6. Write with control of Spanish orthography, accentuation, and punctuation as systems distinct from English.
  7. Design and lead a content-rich instructional session in Spanish, including curating vocabulary from authentic sources and facilitating peer discussion.

The first two weeks are instructor-designed and do two jobs at once: they diagnose what each student actually brings, and they model in full the kind of session students will be asked to run from Week 3 onward.

Week 1 August 24, 26, 28

¿Qué español hablo yo?

Diagnostics, and the sociolinguistics of one's own Spanish. The class opens by making its own linguistic diversity the object of study.

Mon, Aug 24

Autobiografía lingüística

Course launch and the group-presentation contract. Each student delivers a three-minute spoken language autobiography: where the Spanish came from, which country or countries shaped it, which registers they own and which they have never needed. Recorded as the baseline oral sample (Muestra Oral 1). ACTFL Advanced descriptors are introduced so students can locate themselves honestly rather than by self-flattery or self-deprecation.

Wed, Aug 26

Registro y variedad

Direct instruction on register and diatopic variation. Case study one: mission-acquired Spanish as a genuine sociolect — its lexical fingerprints, its usted-heavy and morally-inflected register, its ecclesiastical vocabulary crowding out secular equivalents, and the specific places where it helps and where it betrays. Case study two: heritage Spanish — oral command and pragmatic fluency alongside orthographic and formal-register gaps, and why the 'you speak it wrong' framing is both false and pedagogically destructive. The point of the day is that everyone in the room has an incomplete Spanish, in different places.

Fri, Aug 28

Clínica gramatical 1: Ortografía y acentuación

The accentuation system in full: agudas, llanas, esdrújulas, sobresdrújulas; diptongo, hiato, triptongo; tilde diacrítica; monosyllables; interrogatives and exclamatives in direct and indirect questions. Then the orthographic contrasts that hit heritage writers hardest (b/v, s/c/z, ll/y, h, g/j) and the ones that hit classroom learners hardest (double letters, English-influenced spellings). Diagnostic writing sample collected: 300 words, dictation plus free response (Muestra Escrita 1). Add deadline is today.

Week 2 August 31, September 2, 4

Cómo se construye una clase

The instructor teaches one complete model week so that students see exactly what they are being asked to produce — and so the first student group is not guessing.

Mon, Aug 31

Sesión modelo de contenido

A full group-style content session, run by the instructor, on a sample topic. Suggested: el trabajo agrícola y la migración laboral en Utah — locally grounded, lexically rich, and register-flexible. The session visibly demonstrates the four moves students will have to make: framing a context worth an hour, curating high-value lexical items with regional variants marked, sourcing authentic audio and text, and designing a discussion protocol that produces talk rather than silence.

Wed, Sep 2

Clínica gramatical 2: El pasado narrativo

Pretérito and imperfecto past the 'completed versus ongoing' heuristic: aspectual grounding — foreground and background in narrative; verbs that shift meaning under aspect (supe/sabía, conocí/conocía, quise/quería, pude/podía, tuve/tenía); the imperfect for reported states and habitual frames; the preterite summarizing a bounded state (fue difícil, vivió allí veinte años). Deliberately modeled as the move of pulling grammar out of Monday's content — the same move the instructor will make every Wednesday for the rest of the term.

Fri, Sep 4

Taller de diseño y formación de grupos

Groups form. Topics are pitched, negotiated, and claimed, and the semester calendar is filled in live with students selecting their weeks against the published grammar spine. Rubric norming: students score a sample presentation and calibrate against each other. Vocabulary-curation lab: RAE, Diccionario panhispánico de dudas, Diccionario de americanismos, Corpus del Español — and a frank demonstration of where Google Translate and Linguee mislead at this level.

From Week 3 onward, students own the content and the context of the course. The instructor owns the grammar. The grammatical focus for every week is published in advance, so groups select topics already knowing which structure their week has to feed.

The weekly rhythm

Group assignment

Six groups of three to four students. Each group leads two weeks across Weeks 3 through 14; Week 15 is run collectively by the full cohort as roundtables. Weeks are claimed in Week 2 against the published grammar spine, on a first-committed basis.

Week 3 Sep 9, 11 2 sessions

Ser, estar, haber y el sistema de la pasiva

Focus

Ser versus estar past the adjective list. Estar + participio as resultant state, pasiva con ser as event, pasiva refleja (se construyó) as the unmarked Spanish default. Hay versus está. Ser for the location of events. The real limits of estar + gerundio.

Why this, for this cohort

Experienced speakers usually have serviceable ser/estar but default to English-shaped ser-passives (fue construido) where Spanish overwhelmingly prefers se construyó. This is the single most audible 'translated' tell in otherwise fluent advanced writing.

Friday production task

Rewriting workshop: a journalistic text stripped of its passive strategies and rebuilt three ways, with judgments about which sounds native.

Labor Day, Mon Sep 7 — no classes. Two-session week: content Wednesday, grammar and production compressed Friday.
Week 4 Sep 14, 16, 18 3 sessions

El sistema del se

Focus

Reflexive, reciprocal, pasiva refleja, se impersonal, se de involuntariedad (se me olvidó), and the aspectual or intensifying se (comerse, irse, morirse, saberse). The agency distinction between se me cayó and lo dejé caer — grammar as a way of assigning or dodging blame.

Why this, for this cohort

Heritage speakers own this intuitively but cannot explain or deploy it under formal writing pressure. Classroom and mission learners systematically underuse the aspectual se and overreach with agentive constructions.

Friday production task

Incident narration: the same event told three times, shifting responsibility purely through se-constructions.

Cancellation deadline, Mon Sep 14.
Week 5 Sep 21, 23, 25 3 sessions

Subjuntivo I: cláusulas nominales y secuencia de tiempos

Focus

Volition, emotion, doubt, and denial. El hecho de que. The indicative/subjunctive contrast under creer, no creer, parecer, quizá(s), tal vez. The full sequence-of-tenses grid, including violations under past-tense matrices.

Why this, for this cohort

Mission-acquired Spanish is subjunctive-rich but formulaic — espero que, ojalá, quiero que. The gap is at the margins: emotion verbs with coreferential subjects, el que clauses, and sequence collapse when the matrix verb moves into the past.

Friday production task

Opinion panel on a contested topic drawn from the group's content, structured so that hedged and attributed claims are required.

Week 6 Sep 28, 30, Oct 2 3 sessions

Subjuntivo II: cláusulas adjetivas y adverbiales

Focus

Specificity and existence in relative clauses (busco a alguien que sabe versus que sepa). Adverbial conjunctions that always take subjunctive (para que, antes de que, a menos que, sin que) against those that alternate (cuando, aunque, después de que, mientras, hasta que). Aunque as concession versus hypothesis.

Why this, for this cohort

The alternating adverbials are where advanced speakers still leak, and the leak is meaning-bearing rather than cosmetic — the mood choice changes what is being claimed about the world.

Friday production task

Contingency planning simulation: a scenario requiring conditions, purposes, and time clauses under genuine uncertainty.

Week 7 Oct 5, 7 2 sessions

Imperfecto de subjuntivo, condicional y las cláusulas con si

Focus

All three si-clause types, plus como si and ojalá with imperfecto and pluscuamperfecto de subjuntivo. The -ra versus -se alternation and its regional distribution. The courtesy conditional (quisiera, debiera, podría). Counterfactual regret.

Why this, for this cohort

This is the register hinge of the course. Hypothetical and counterfactual reasoning is precisely what separates Advanced from Intermediate in the ACTFL descriptors, and it is where acquired fluency thins out fastest — speakers who never had to argue counterfactually in the field simply never built it.

Friday production task

Counterfactual history or counterfactual autobiography: what would have happened if.

No classes Fri Oct 9. Two-session week: content Monday, grammar and production compressed Wednesday.
Week 8 Oct 12, 14, 16 3 sessions

Pronombres átonos, duplicación y variación dialectal

Focus

Clitic order and placement. Clitic doubling (a mí me gusta; lo vi a Juan in Rioplatense). Leísmo, laísmo, and loísmo as regional norms rather than errors. Lo neutro (lo bueno, lo que). Pronominal verbs with dative experiencers: me falta, me sobra, me cuesta, me toca, me da igual.

Why this, for this cohort

The strongest hook of the semester for regional variation. Students who served in Spain and students who served in Peru will disagree in the room about what is correct — and both will be right. That disagreement is the lesson.

Friday production task

Dialect panel: students defend the usage of their own acquisition country against the others, with evidence from corpora rather than intuition.

Week 9 Oct 19, 21, 23 3 sessions

Preposiciones, régimen verbal y los calcos

Focus

Por and para as a system rather than a memorized list. Verbs with obligatory prepositions: soñar con, contar con, insistir en, quedar en, tratar de versus tratarse de, depender de, consistir en. The personal a beyond simple animacy. A targeted audit of high-frequency English calques: aplicar para, asistir, atender, realizar, soportar, introducir, moverse, registrarse, estar supuesto a.

Why this, for this cohort

The highest-yield single week of the term for mission-acquired Spanish. These errors are fossilized precisely because they are socially invisible inside a missionary speech community — everyone said aplicar, so it never got corrected — and they are conspicuous the moment the speaker enters an academic or professional Spanish-language setting.

Friday production task

Personal calque inventory: each student audits their own recorded Week 1 sample and builds a private error list to carry into the portfolio.

Week 10 Oct 26, 28, 30 3 sessions

Oraciones de relativo y subordinación compleja

Focus

Que, quien, el que, el cual, cuyo. Restrictive versus non-restrictive relatives and what the comma actually does. Preposition plus relative pronoun. Reduced clauses with participles and gerunds. Building forty-word sentences that stay grammatical to the end.

Why this, for this cohort

The move from Advanced-Low to Advanced-Mid is largely a matter of syntactic density. Oral fluency does not produce this on its own — speakers with excellent conversational Spanish often top out at short coordinate sentences. It has to be taught explicitly.

Friday production task

Sentence-combining laboratory, then a formal written abstract of the group's topic under a sentence-length floor.

Week 11 Nov 2, 4, 6 3 sessions

Registro, conectores y nominalización

Focus

Discourse markers organized by function: addition, contrast, cause, consequence, concession, reformulation. Nominalization as the engine of formal prose (decidieron becomes la decisión de). The address systems in full — tú, usted, vos, vosotros, ustedes — including voseo morphology in Argentina, Uruguay, Central America, and paisa Colombia, and an honest account of where vosotros actually lives.

Why this, for this cohort

Address systems are the most direct payoff of the regional thread, and experienced speakers frequently carry one country's system as though it were the language itself. A returned missionary from Madrid and one from Buenos Aires have incompatible defaults, and neither has usually been told so.

Friday production task

Register transposition: one message rewritten for five audiences across three countries.

Withdrawal deadline, Tue Nov 3.
Week 12 Nov 9, 11, 13 3 sessions

Discurso referido y atribución

Focus

Reported speech and tense backshift. Dijo que versus dice que. Subjunctive under reported commands. Attribution and hedging verbs: sostener, afirmar, señalar, alegar, matizar, reconocer, desmentir. The reportative conditional in journalism (habría sido).

Why this, for this cohort

This is the grammar of citing a source, which every group presentation already requires. It is also the structural core of academic and professional register — the ability to report a claim without endorsing it.

Friday production task

Press-review exercise: the same event as reported by outlets from three countries, with attribution strategies compared.

Week 13 Nov 16, 18, 20 3 sessions

Tiempos compuestos y modalidad epistémica

Focus

The full compound paradigm: he, había, habré, habría, haya, hubiera plus participle. Pretérito perfecto compuesto versus indefinido and its sharp peninsular/American split. Future and conditional of probability (serán las tres; estaría enfermo). Deber de versus deber.

Why this, for this cohort

The perfect/preterite split is the cleanest peninsular-versus-American contrast available, and it lands unusually well with a group whose members acquired the language in different countries and have never had the difference named for them.

Friday production task

Speculation task: students reason aloud about an ambiguous scenario using only epistemic future and conditional forms.

Week 14 Nov 23, 25 2 sessions

Taller de escritura: ortografía, puntuación y pulido

Focus

Spanish punctuation as a system in its own right: inverted opening marks, raya de diálogo, comillas latinas, comma conventions that diverge from English, and the non-capitalization of nationalities, months, days, and languages. Accentuation review under time pressure. Each student assembles a personal error inventory from the semester's accumulated writing.

Why this, for this cohort

Placed deliberately late. It works far better as a revision week feeding the portfolio than as a front-loaded rules lecture that students forget by October.

Friday production task

Portfolio revision workshop with structured peer editing against personal error inventories.

Thanksgiving, Thu–Fri Nov 26–27 — no classes. Two-session week.
Week 15 Nov 30, Dec 2, 4 3 sessions

Síntesis y mesas redondas

Focus

No new grammar. Student-run roundtables synthesizing the semester's topics, with the whole cohort facilitating rather than a single group. Final oral sample recorded (Muestra Oral 3) and set directly against the Week 1 recording.

Why this, for this cohort

The comparison to the Week 1 baseline is the assessment. Students who have been told all semester that their Spanish is already good need concrete evidence of what actually changed.

Friday production task

Portfolio due. Final self-assessment against the ACTFL descriptors introduced in Week 1.

Last day of classes, Fri Dec 4.

Fifteen instructional weeks, forty-two class meetings. Three weeks lose a session to a university closure and are marked below; those weeks compress the content and grammar sessions rather than dropping the grammatical target.

Materials

No textbook purchase is required. The reference set below is free or library-accessible, and the weekly content is sourced by student groups from authentic media.

Nueva gramática básica de la lengua española
Real Academia Española — the standing grammar reference for the clinics.
Diccionario panhispánico de dudas
RAE/ASALE, free online. The default arbiter for usage disputes, including regional ones.
Diccionario de americanismos
ASALE, free online. Essential for the regional-variation thread.
Corpus del Español
Mark Davies. Used to settle frequency and regional-distribution questions with evidence rather than intuition.
Authentic media
Sourced weekly by the presenting group: broadcast, print, podcast, and social media from the relevant country or countries.

Presente simple vs. progresivo

Narrar en vivo

Two picture stories, one grammar trap: knowing when a moment calls for estar + gerundio and when plain simple present already has the job covered.

Quick recap before you start — simple present does three jobs, progressive does one:

1. Narrated instant (play-by-play): simple present, no estar — "¡Dispara! ¡Gooool!", "El hombre roba el maletín."
2. Habitual routine / general fact: simple present — "Entrena todas las mañanas.", "Actúa de noche."
3. Genuinely mid-action, caught in the moment: estar + gerundio — "El hombre está saltando la valla.", "El estadio está celebrando."

The trap: instinct says "it's happening right now, so use progressive." Spanish disagrees for play-by-play and routines. Progressive is for the third case only.

El robo

Conversación en pareja

Your task

With a partner, go panel by panel. For each one, decide out loud: would a witness describing this as it happens reach for estar + gerundio, or would a police report narrating events in sequence stay in simple present? Build at least one full sentence per panel, in Spanish, and be ready to defend your choice.

1

The café, 20:15:42. What is he doing to her briefcase right now, while she's distracted at the counter?

Le está robando el maletín a la mujer mientras ella no mira. (caught mid-action → progresivo)
2

The fence, five in the morning. What's he doing to get over it, and what fell out of his bag?

Está saltando la valla y se le caen los papeles de la mochila. (unfolding as you watch → progresivo)
3

The gallery, 2:00 AM. What is he doing to the painting — and what is the guard doing, or not doing?

Está descolgando el cuadro de la pared. El vigilante duerme en la silla. (the thief: progresivo; the guard: simple present — a state, not an action underway)
4

The dock, in the rain. What are the two of them doing with that envelope as the boat pulls in?

Están intercambiando un sobre bajo la lluvia mientras llega el barco. (progresivo)

El partido

Conversación en pareja

Your task

This one is the trap in reverse. A sports commentator narrating live still uses simple present, goal by goal — that's convention, not a grammar mistake. Go panel by panel and decide which of the three jobs from the recap above each one is doing, then build a sentence in Spanish that fits.

1

Try this one like a real commentator: fast, present tense, no estar. What does Gutiérrez do, step by step, up to the goal?

Gutiérrez driblea, dispara... ¡y gol! (play-by-play → simple present, even though it's live)
2

5 a.m. What does he do every morning, and who trains with him?

Todas las mañanas corre y entrena con su abuela. (habitual routine → simple present)
3

Now describe this one the way you'd describe a photo you're looking at right now, not a highlight reel. What is the stadium doing?

El estadio entero está celebrando. (genuinely mid-unfolding → progresivo)
4

What has the coach been doing for the last thirty minutes, and how would you describe him at this exact instant?

Lleva media hora gesticulando sin sentarse. Ahora mismo está gritando algo al árbitro. (duration → llevar + gerundio; the instant → progresivo — two different tools, watch the overlap)

Ensayo en parejas · 8–10 min

  1. Plan in Spanish. Ask what your partner has tried (¿Has probado la birria?). Say what you’ll order and what you’ll do if your first choice is unavailable (Si no tienen pastor, pediré de asada).
  2. Order aloud. Take turns as customer and server. Greet each other, order at least two items, customize one, respond to the situation, then confirm the total and how you’ll pay. At the truck, include quantities and taco fillings. At the restaurant, order from the dishes below. The server asks at least two follow-up questions. Pick a different situation when you switch roles.
  3. Report back. After both turns, tell another pair what you’ve ordered and what changed. Start with Hemos pedido… and No hemos pedido…

Lo que hemos hecho

The present perfect connects a completed action or experience with the present: Hemos probado los tacos de birria (we have tried the birria tacos). The result matters now. Use it to discuss experiences, changes, or actions within a time period connected to now; regional preferences vary.

Present tense of haber + past participle. Only haber changes with the subject.

El orden de las palabras

No hemos pedido todavía. Nos hemos sentado cerca de la ventana. ¿Has visto el menú? Put no and object/reflexive pronouns before haber. Keep the auxiliary and participle together.

Watch out: Hemos pedido, never hemos pedidos. With haber, the participle does not agree with gender or number. Use he, not soy or tengo, as the auxiliary.

¿Qué has hecho este año?

Ask a partner about three experiences with tacos or restaurants. Follow up with a detail question: ¿Has probado los tacos de lengua? ¿Dónde los probaste? A specific completed past event can use the preterite.

Raíces irregulares

poner → pondr- · tener → tendr- · salir → saldr- · haber → habr- · caber → cabr- · querer → querr- · decir → dir- · hacer → har-. Also poder → podr-; saber → sabr-; venir → vendr-. Add the ordinary future endings. Tendremos que hacer fila. ¿Qué dirán cuando lleguemos? Haré el pedido. Podremos pagar con tarjeta.

Dos maneras de hablar del futuro

Vamos a pedir tacos means “we are going to order tacos”; pediremos tacos means “we will order tacos.” The forms often overlap. Ir a + infinitive commonly presents a plan or a future event connected to the present.

La probabilidad en el presente

¿Qué llevará esta salsa? Llevará chile de árbol. Here the future expresses a guess about now: “What might be in this salsa? It probably has chile de árbol.” Context distinguishes a prediction from a present-time guess.

Watch out: Keep the infinitive: pagaré, not pagué (I paid). For impersonal haber, use singular habrá: Habrá opciones vegetarianas. In a realistic si clause, use present + future: Si no tienen pastor, pediré de asada.

¿Qué pediremos?

With a partner, make three predictions about next week’s visit and one commitment. Ask ¿Qué pedirás? ¿Cuánto costará? ¿Habrá fila? Then make a backup plan with si.
